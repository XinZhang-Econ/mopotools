# Transmission Channels of Quantitative Easing

**Summary**: QE affects the economy through multiple channels including portfolio balance effects, signaling about future policy, liquidity provision, and bank capital effects. The relative importance of each channel varies by context and program design.

**Research classification**: Both (theoretical frameworks and empirical evidence)

**Sources**: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md, the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md, ecbwp1956-pdf.md, qe-the-story-so-far-pdf.md

**Last updated**: 2026-04-21

## Overview

[[quantitative-easing]] affects the economy through several transmission mechanisms. Understanding these channels helps explain why QE works "in practice but not in theory" (as Bernanke noted) and why effectiveness varies across countries and time periods.

## The Irrelevance Proposition

In standard frictionless models (Wallace 1981, Eggertsson and Woodford 2003), QE should have no effect because:
1. With perfect asset substitutability, investors simply undo central bank purchases
2. Modigliani-Miller logic applies: transferring risk from private to public sector doesn't change required returns if taxes adjust

QE "works" because these conditions don't hold in practice. (source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

## Channel 1: Portfolio Balance / Duration Risk

### Mechanism

When the central bank purchases long-term bonds:
1. It removes **duration risk** from private portfolios
2. Investors who sold bonds hold "excess" money balances
3. They rebalance by purchasing other assets (corporate bonds, equities, real estate)
4. Prices rise and yields fall across asset classes
5. Lower borrowing costs and wealth effects stimulate spending

This channel requires **market segmentation** - investors cannot perfectly arbitrage away the central bank's impact. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### Evidence

Bernanke (2012) cited Federal Reserve research suggesting portfolio balance effects accounted for a significant portion of yield reductions from QE. Event studies find yields react immediately to purchase announcements, consistent with portfolio rebalancing. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### Theoretical Foundations

- Tobin (1969): Imperfect asset substitutability
- Brunner and Meltzer (1987): Portfolio balance models
- Vayanos and Vila (2009): Preferred habitat model
- Gertler and Karadi (2011, 2013): Leverage-constrained intermediaries

## Channel 2: Signaling

### Mechanism

Asset purchases signal the central bank's intention to keep policy rates low for an extended period. This works through:

1. **Commitment effect**: Large bond holdings expose the central bank to losses if it raises rates, creating an incentive to maintain low rates
2. **Communication**: Purchases reinforce [[forward-guidance]] about the future policy path
3. **Expectation adjustment**: Long-term rates fall as expected future short rates decline

(source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md)

### Evidence

Mixed. In the UK, studies found the signaling channel played a relatively minor role compared to portfolio balance effects. Expected future short rates did not decline much after QE announcements. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

In the euro area, the ECB APP was associated with both declining expected future short rates and rising inflation expectations, consistent with signaling effects. (source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md)

## Channel 3: Liquidity

### Mechanism

During periods of market stress, liquidity premia spike as investors demand compensation for holding illiquid assets. Central bank purchases can:

1. Improve market functioning by providing a ready buyer
2. Reduce bid-ask spreads and liquidity premia
3. Restore confidence in market access

(source: qe-the-story-so-far-pdf.md)

### Evidence

Particularly relevant for the ECB's early crisis interventions. De Pooter et al. (2015) found the Securities Markets Programme reduced liquidity premia by 32-40 basis points. Krishnamurthy et al. (2018) attributed 50% of the OMT/SMP effect to improved market liquidity. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## Channel 4: Bank Capital Relief

### Mechanism

When central bank purchases raise bond prices:
1. Banks holding those bonds see portfolio gains
2. This improves bank capital ratios
3. Better-capitalized banks can extend more credit
4. Lending rates decline, stimulating investment

This is sometimes called the "bank lending channel" operating through the asset side. (source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md)

### Evidence

Andrade et al. (2016) found that banks with larger government bond holdings experienced greater stock price increases after the ECB APP announcement, consistent with the capital relief mechanism. (source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md)

## Channel 5: Broad Money / Buffer Stock

### Mechanism (UK Model)

The Bank of England emphasized a distinctly "monetarist" transmission:

1. QE creates broad money (M4) when central bank purchases gilts from non-banks
2. Non-bank financial companies (pension funds, insurers) hold excess deposits
3. These institutions rebalance portfolios, bidding up asset prices
4. Eventually money flows to households and corporates as spending increases
5. The "hot potato" effect passes money through the economy until spending rises enough to restore money demand equilibrium

(source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

### Key Distinction

This differs from the standard portfolio balance story in emphasizing the **quantity of money** rather than the **removal of duration risk**. Both mechanisms may operate simultaneously.

## Channel 6: Exchange Rate

### Mechanism

QE can depreciate the currency through:
1. Lower domestic yields make foreign assets more attractive
2. Portfolio outflows as investors rebalance internationally
3. Capital account effects

This boosts net exports and imported inflation.

### Evidence

Exchange rate effects have been observed but are complicated when multiple central banks pursue QE simultaneously (offsetting effects). Japan's yen depreciation during Abenomics was substantial. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## Channel 7: Confidence / Uncertainty

### Mechanism

QE may reduce economic uncertainty by:
1. Signaling central bank commitment to act
2. Reducing tail risks (deflation, financial collapse)
3. Boosting business and consumer confidence

Weale and Wieladek (2016) found UK QE had negligible yield effects but stimulated the economy by reducing uncertainty. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## Channel 8: Reanchoring (ECB)

### Mechanism

If long-term inflation expectations have drifted below target, QE may:
1. Signal commitment to achieving target
2. Raise short-term inflation expectations
3. Gradually pull long-term expectations back toward target

The ECB APP appeared to reverse declines in long-term inflation expectations observed during 2014. (source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md)

## Relative Importance Across Countries

| Channel | US | UK | Euro Area | Japan | Sweden |
|---------|----|----|-----------|-------|--------|
| Portfolio Balance | High | High | High | Moderate | Moderate |
| Signaling | Moderate | Low | Moderate | Low | High |
| Liquidity | High (QE1) | Moderate | Very High (SMP/OMT) | Low | Moderate |
| Bank Capital | Low | Low | High | Moderate | Low |
| Broad Money | Low | High | Moderate | Low | Low |
| Exchange Rate | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | High | **Very High** |
| Reanchoring | Low | Low | High | Low | Moderate |
| Cash Flow | Low | Moderate | Low | Low | **High** |

### Sweden: A Small Open Economy Case

Sweden provides a distinctive case study because as a small, open economy with a floating exchange rate, the **exchange rate channel** dominates:

1. **Exchange Rate Channel**: Kolasa, Laséen & Lindé (2025) find QE was effective largely because it led to real depreciation of the krona. This stimulated exports and raised import prices, accounting for most of the inflation impact. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

2. **Cash Flow Channel**: Particularly strong in Sweden given the predominance of variable-rate mortgages. Flodén et al (2021) document that indebted households' consumption declines significantly more in response to policy rate changes. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

3. **Signaling Channel**: Forward guidance was notably effective in Sweden. Almerud et al (2024) find forward guidance shocks are close substitutes for actual rate changes. The Riksbank's practice of publishing policy rate paths since 2007 reinforced the understanding that forecasts were conditional, helping avoid time-inconsistency problems. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

## Implications for Policy Design

1. **Asset type matters**: Purchasing riskier/longer-duration assets has larger portfolio balance effects
2. **Market conditions matter**: Liquidity channel is strongest during stress
3. **Bank structure matters**: Capital relief channel is stronger in bank-dependent economies
4. **Communication matters**: Signaling channel requires clear forward guidance
5. **Credibility matters**: Reanchoring only works if markets believe the central bank
