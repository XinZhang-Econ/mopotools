# Quantitative Easing: United States

**Summary**: The Federal Reserve implemented three rounds of large-scale asset purchases (LSAPs) between 2008-2014, totaling approximately $4.5 trillion USD (~25% of GDP) in Treasury securities and mortgage-backed securities.

**Research classification**: Empirical

**Sources**: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md, four-stories-of-quantitative-easing-pdf.md, ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md, fifty-shades-of-qe-pdf.md

**Last updated**: 2026-04-21

---

## Background

The US entered the Global Financial Crisis with the federal funds rate at 5.25%. By December 2008, the Fed had cut rates to near zero (0-0.25%), reaching the [[zero-lower-bound]]. With conventional policy exhausted, the Fed turned to unconventional tools. (source: four-stories-of-quantitative-easing-pdf.md)

## QE Programs

### QE1 (November 2008 - March 2010)

**Announced**: November 25, 2008
**Total purchases**: $1.725 trillion USD (~12% of 2009 GDP)
- $1.25 trillion USD in agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS)
- $175 billion USD in agency debt
- $300 billion USD in longer-term Treasury securities

**Rationale**: Address dysfunction in credit markets and support housing finance. Initially focused on MBS to directly target the source of the crisis. (source: four-stories-of-quantitative-easing-pdf.md)

**Effects on yields**: Bernanke (2012) reported estimates of 40-110 basis point reduction in 10-year Treasury yields. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### QE2 (November 2010 - June 2011)

**Announced**: November 3, 2010
**Total purchases**: $600 billion USD (~4% of GDP) in longer-term Treasury securities

**Rationale**: Provide additional monetary accommodation given slow recovery and below-target inflation. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

**Effects on yields**: Estimated 15-45 basis point reduction in 10-year yields. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### Operation Twist / MEP (September 2011 - December 2012)

**Maturity Extension Program**: Sold $667 billion USD in short-term Treasuries and purchased equivalent amount in long-term Treasuries.

**Rationale**: Flatten the yield curve without expanding the balance sheet. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### QE3 (September 2012 - October 2014)

**Announced**: September 13, 2012
**Monthly purchases**: $40 billion USD MBS initially, expanded to $85 billion USD/month (including $45 billion USD Treasuries) in December 2012

**Key innovation**: Open-ended commitment - purchases would continue until labor market improved substantially, rather than a fixed amount.

**Tapering**: Began January 2014, concluded October 2014.

## Macroeconomic Effects

### Effects on Yields

| Study | Program | 10-Year Yield Impact |
|-------|---------|---------------------|
| Gagnon et al. (2011) | QE1 | -38 to -82 bps |
| Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2011) | QE1 | -107 bps |
| Hamilton & Wu (2012) | QE1 | -13 bps |
| Bernanke (2012) | QE1 | -40 to -110 bps |
| Bernanke (2012) | QE2 | -15 to -45 bps |

The wide range reflects methodological differences. See [[research-debates]].

### Effects on GDP and Employment

Bernanke (2012) cited model estimates suggesting QE raised real GDP by approximately 3% and increased private payroll employment by more than 2 million jobs relative to a baseline without asset purchases. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### Counterfactual Analysis

Baumeister and Benati (2013) used a time-varying parameter VAR to simulate what would have happened without QE:
- GDP growth would have contracted by 10% (annualized) in 2009Q1
- The economy would have experienced deflation of -1% in 2009Q2
- Unemployment would have exceeded 10.6% by end of 2009

Their conclusion: QE "averted significant risks both of deflation and of output collapses comparable to those that took place during the Great Depression." (source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)

### Effects on Inflation

Inflation remained subdued despite massive balance sheet expansion, contrary to some critics' predictions. Core PCE inflation averaged around 1.5% during QE, below the Fed's 2% target.

## Transmission Channels

The Fed's QE operated primarily through:

1. **Portfolio balance channel**: Removing duration risk from private portfolios
2. **Signaling channel**: Communicating commitment to keep rates low
3. **Wealth effects**: Higher asset prices supporting consumption
4. **Mortgage rate channel**: Direct purchases of MBS reduced mortgage rates

Unlike the UK approach, the Fed's QE focused less on the money supply channel since purchases were often from primary dealers rather than non-bank investors. (source: four-stories-of-quantitative-easing-pdf.md)

## Key Figures

- **Ben Bernanke** - Fed Chair 2006-2014, architect of QE programs
- **Janet Yellen** - Fed Chair 2014-2018, oversaw QE exit
