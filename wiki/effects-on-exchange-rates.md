# Effects of QE on Exchange Rates

**Summary**: QE tends to depreciate the domestic currency by lowering yields relative to other countries, making foreign assets more attractive. Exchange rate effects have been significant but are complicated when multiple central banks pursue QE simultaneously.

**Research classification**: Empirical

**Sources**: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018-unconventional-monetary-policies-in-the-euro-area-japan-and-the-united-kingd.md, the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md

**Last updated**: 2026-04-21

---

## Mechanism

[[quantitative-easing]] affects exchange rates through:

1. **Interest rate differential**: Lower domestic yields make foreign assets more attractive
2. **Portfolio outflows**: Investors rebalance toward foreign currency assets
3. **Capital flows**: "Search for yield" pushes capital abroad
4. **Signaling**: Commitment to easy policy weighs on currency

## Country Experiences

### United States

The dollar's response to Fed QE was mixed:
- Initial weakness during QE1 as rates fell
- Subsequent strength as US recovery led other economies
- Global reserve currency status complicates interpretation

Spillover effects to emerging markets were significant - EM currencies appreciated as capital flowed seeking yield.

### United Kingdom

Sterling depreciated substantially during the crisis:
- ~25% depreciation 2007-2009
- Depreciation occurred largely before QE announcement
- Relatively stable during QE period itself

Bridges and Thomas (2012) noted the exchange rate channel was one hypothesized transmission mechanism, but sterling remained "relatively stable during the QE period and has remained broadly flat since." (source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

### Euro Area

The euro depreciated approximately 30% versus USD between 2013-2017:
- Hutchinson and Smets (2017) attribute this partly to ECB unconventional policies
- Euro weakness boosted export competitiveness
- Import price inflation was modest given low commodity prices

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Japan

The yen experienced substantial depreciation during Abenomics:
- Sharp yen weakness in late 2012 as Abe's election and policy intentions became clear
- Much of the depreciation occurred before formal QQE announcement
- Contributed to import price inflation

This was one of the more successful QE transmission channels for Japan, boosting export profits and raising import prices. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## "Currency Wars" Concerns

Critics argued QE was "beggar-thy-neighbor" policy:
- Depreciation shifts demand toward domestic goods
- Other countries bear the cost
- Competitive devaluations could escalate

### Counterarguments

1. **Global stimulus**: If QE raises domestic demand, imports rise too
2. **Synchronized policy**: When all major central banks pursue QE, effects offset
3. **Financial stability**: QE spillovers to EM provided cheap financing
4. **Better than deflation**: Alternative was worse global recession

## Spillovers to Emerging Markets

QE in advanced economies affected EM:

### Capital Inflows
- "Search for yield" pushed capital into EM
- EM currencies appreciated
- EM asset prices rose

### Policy Dilemmas
- EM central banks faced difficult choices
- Tighten to control inflows? (slows growth)
- Maintain easy policy? (risks bubbles)

### "Taper Tantrum" (2013)
- When Fed signaled tapering, EM experienced sudden outflows
- EM currencies and asset prices fell sharply
- Highlighted fragility of QE spillover flows

## Limitations

### Offsetting Policies

When multiple central banks pursue QE:
- Effects on bilateral rates may offset
- Dollar-euro-yen relationships depend on relative policy stance
- Trade-weighted effects smaller than bilateral

### Market Functioning

During crisis periods, exchange rate effects may be muted:
- Safe haven flows complicate interpretation
- Dollar strength despite Fed easing reflects flight to safety
