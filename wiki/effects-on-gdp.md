# Effects of QE on GDP and Output

**Summary**: Empirical studies find QE raised GDP by 1-3% (peak effect relative to baseline) depending on the program and country. Effects operate through lower borrowing costs, wealth effects, and exchange rate depreciation. Counterfactual simulations suggest QE prevented significantly worse outcomes during the Great Recession.

**Research classification**: Empirical

**Sources**: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md, ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md, the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md, dell-ariccia-et-al-2018-unconventional-monetary-policies-in-the-euro-area-japan-and-the-united-kingd.md

## Overview

While [[effects-on-yields]] are the most directly measurable impact of [[quantitative-easing]], the ultimate goal is to stimulate real economic activity. Measuring GDP effects is more challenging because macro variables move slowly and many factors affect output simultaneously.

## Transmission to GDP

QE affects output through several channels:

1. **Lower borrowing costs**: Reduced yields make investment and consumption cheaper
2. **Wealth effects**: Higher asset prices increase household wealth and spending
3. **Bank lending**: Improved bank capital positions enable more lending
4. **Exchange rate**: Currency depreciation boosts net exports
5. **Confidence**: Reduced uncertainty may boost business investment

See [[transmission-channels]] for details.

## United States

### Model-Based Estimates

Bernanke (2012) cited Federal Reserve research suggesting:
- QE raised real GDP by approximately **3%** relative to baseline
- Increased private payroll employment by more than **2 million jobs**

(source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### Counterfactual Analysis

Baumeister and Benati (2013) used a time-varying parameter VAR to simulate what would have happened without QE. They found that in the absence of the Fed's asset purchases:

- GDP growth would have contracted by **-10% (annualized)** in 2009Q1
- Unemployment would have exceeded **10.6%** by end of 2009
- The economy risked "output collapses comparable to those that took place during the Great Depression"

The 16th percentile of their counterfactual distribution showed output contracting by **20%** - comparable to Great Depression rates. (source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)

## United Kingdom

### Estimates

| Study | Method | GDP Impact |
|-------|--------|------------|
| Bridges & Thomas (2012) | Monetarist model | +2% peak |
| Kapetanios et al. (2012) | VAR | +1.5% |
| Baumeister & Benati (2013) | TVP-VAR | +3% |
| Churm et al. (2015) | VAR | +0.5-0.8% (QE2/QE3) |
| Weale & Wieladek (2016) | VAR | +0.25% per 1% of GDP in purchases |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### The Monetarist Arithmetic

Bridges and Thomas (2012) traced the transmission:
1. QE increased broad money supply by 8%
2. This reduced yields by ~150 bps
3. Asset values rose ~20%
4. Peak GDP impact of 2% by mid-2011

They noted this was "illustrative arithmetic" given unprecedented nature of QE. (source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

### Counterfactual

Baumeister and Benati (2013) found that without BoE QE:
- Output growth would have dropped by **12% at annual rates**
- The economy faced severe contraction risk

(source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)

## Euro Area

### Estimates

| Study | Program | GDP Impact |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Altavilla et al. (2014) | OMT | +1.5% (Italy), +2% (Spain) |
| Andrade et al. (2016) | APP | +1.1% |
| Darracq-Paries & De Santis (2015) | 3-year LTRO | +0.8% |
| Cova et al. (2015) | APP | +1.4% |
| Hutchinson & Smets (2017) | Combined | +1.7% (cumulative 2016-19) |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Cross-Country Effects

Effects varied across member states:
- Peripheral countries (Italy, Spain) benefited more from yield compression
- Core countries (Germany, France) still benefited through trade linkages
- The OMT announcement alone stimulated activity without actual purchases

## Japan

### Estimates

| Study | Program | GDP Impact |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Hausman & Wieland (2014) | QQE1 | up to +1% |
| Kan et al. (2016) | QQE1 | +0.6% to +4.2% |
| Michaelis & Watzka (2017) | QQE | +0.2% per 1% increase in reserves |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Why More Modest?

Japan's effects appear smaller because:
- Yields were already very low
- Deflationary expectations were entrenched
- Structural headwinds (demographics) constrained growth
- Central bank credibility was lower

See [[qe-japan]] for discussion.

## Sweden

### Estimates

| Period | Program | GDP Effect | Source |
|--------|---------|-----------|--------|
| 2015-2019 | Negative rates + QE | ~1% boost | Kolasa, Laséen & Lindé (2025) |
| 2020-2023 | Covid-era QE | ~0.2% average | Akkaya et al (2023) |

The larger effects during 2015-2019 reflect:
- Exchange rate depreciation (krona weakened, boosting exports)
- More distressed market conditions initially
- Longer duration of the program

Covid-era QE had more modest effects because:
- Asset composition weighted toward covered bonds (weaker transmission)
- Purchases were smaller relative to economy
- Market stress had already subsided by the time some programs were implemented

(source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

### Estimates of Conventional Monetary Policy Effects

Studies of conventional monetary policy transmission in Sweden (effects of 1 percentage point policy rate increase):

| Study | Method | GDP Effect | Peak Timing |
|-------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Berggren et al (2024) | Bayesian VAR | -0.7% to -0.8% | ~2 years |
| Almerud et al (2024) | High-frequency + Proxy SVAR | -1.5% | ~2 years |
| Coglianese et al (2025) | Narrative (2010-11 episode) | -5% | ~2.5 years |

The wide range reflects methodological differences. Coglianese et al's larger estimate draws on the 2010-11 tightening episode when the Riksbank raised rates primarily for financial stability reasons. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

See [[qe-sweden]] for details.

## Mechanisms of GDP Impact

### Investment Channel

Lower yields reduce the cost of capital, boosting business investment:
- Corporate bond yields decline
- Bank lending rates fall
- Equity financing becomes cheaper (higher stock prices)

### Consumption Channel

Higher asset prices boost household wealth:
- Housing wealth effects
- Portfolio gains on retirement savings
- Consumer confidence improves

### Credit Channel

Improved bank capital positions enable more lending:
- Banks holding bonds see portfolio gains
- Capital ratios improve
- Lending capacity expands

However, this channel was impaired in the UK and euro area where banks were deleveraging.

### Net Export Channel

Currency depreciation boosts export competitiveness:
- Japan saw significant yen depreciation during Abenomics
- Effects dampened when all major economies pursue QE simultaneously

## Employment Effects

QE also affected labor markets:

### United States
- Bernanke (2012): 2+ million jobs added
- Chung et al. (2012): Unemployment 0.75 percentage points lower

### United Kingdom
- GDP effects imply substantial job creation
- Exact employment impact harder to isolate

## Caveats

### Identification Challenges

GDP effects are difficult to identify because:
- QE was deployed during extraordinary circumstances
- Multiple policies operated simultaneously
- Counterfactual is unknowable
- Model dependence is high

### Diminishing Returns?

Some evidence suggests later QE rounds had smaller effects:
- Markets more distressed in 2008-09 (larger treatment effect)
- Later rounds may have been anticipated
- But may also reflect smaller "surprise" rather than true diminishing returns

### Time-Varying Effects

Baumeister and Benati (2013) emphasized that fixed-coefficient models underestimate effects during crisis:
- Economic relationships changed during the Great Recession
- TVP models find larger effects in recent period

(source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)
