# Quantitative Easing: United Kingdom

**Summary**: The Bank of England implemented four rounds of quantitative easing through its Asset Purchase Facility (APF) between 2009-2016, purchasing £435 billion GBP (~25% of GDP) in gilts and £10 billion GBP in corporate bonds.

**Research classification**: Empirical

**Sources**: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md, qe-the-story-so-far-pdf.md, dell-ariccia-et-al-2018-unconventional-monetary-policies-in-the-euro-area-japan-and-the-united-kingd.md, ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md

## Background

The UK economy contracted by approximately 6% from peak to trough during the Global Financial Crisis. Bank Rate was cut from 5.75% to 0.5% by March 2009. With rates at the effective lower bound, the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) launched quantitative easing. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## QE Programs

### QE1 (March 2009 - January 2010)

**Announced**: March 5, 2009
**Initial target**: £75 billion GBP
**Final purchases**: £200 billion GBP (approximately 13% of 2009 GDP)

**Assets purchased**: Predominantly gilt-edged securities (UK government bonds)

**Rationale**: Boost nominal spending and offset deflationary pressures when Bank Rate could not be cut further. (source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

### QE2 (October 2011 - May 2012)

**Announced**: October 6, 2011
**Additional purchases**: £75 billion GBP, later expanded to £125 billion GBP

**Context**: Euro-area sovereign debt crisis threatened UK economic outlook. Despite inflation running above target (due to VAT increases and energy prices), the MPC judged downside risks warranted further stimulus.

### QE3 (July 2012 - November 2012)

**Announced**: July 5, 2012
**Additional purchases**: £50 billion GBP

**Total APF holdings**: £375 billion GBP (approximately 25% of GDP, 35% of outstanding gilt stock)

### QE4 (August 2016)

**Announced**: August 4, 2016 (following Brexit referendum)
**Additional purchases**: £60 billion GBP gilts + £10 billion GBP corporate bonds
**Bank Rate cut**: From 0.5% to 0.25%

**Rationale**: Support economic growth following uncertainty from EU referendum result.

## The UK "Monetarist" Approach

The Bank of England's QE was distinctive in emphasizing the **broad money channel**. Purchases were explicitly targeted at non-bank financial institutions (pension funds, insurance companies, asset managers) rather than banks. (source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

### Transmission Mechanism

1. **Initial impact**: BoE purchases gilts from non-bank private sector
2. **Money creation**: Creates bank deposits (broad money M4) for the sellers
3. **Portfolio rebalancing**: Financial companies holding excess money seek higher-yielding assets
4. **Asset price effects**: Prices rise / yields fall across asset classes
5. **Spending effects**: Higher wealth and lower borrowing costs stimulate consumption and investment
6. **Sectoral money flows**: Money flows from financial sector to households and corporates as spending increases

### Money Supply Effects

Bridges and Thomas (2012) estimated that £200 billion GBP of QE1:
- Boosted broad money supply by approximately £122 billion GBP (8% of M4)
- Some offset from banks issuing long-term debt and equity to repair balance sheets
- Net money shock was smaller than gross purchases due to "leakages"

(source: the-impact-of-qe-on-the-uk-economy-some-supportive-monetarist-arithmetic-pdf.md)

## Macroeconomic Effects

### Effects on Yields

| Study | Program | 10-Year Yield Impact |
|-------|---------|---------------------|
| Joyce et al. (2011) | QE1 £200bn | -100 bps |
| Christensen & Rudebusch (2012) | QE1/QE2 £275bn | -47 bps |
| Bridges & Thomas (2012) | QE1 £200bn | -150 bps |

The portfolio balance channel appears dominant; the signaling channel played a smaller role. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Effects on GDP

| Study | Method | GDP Impact |
|-------|--------|------------|
| Bridges & Thomas (2012) | Monetarist model | +2% peak |
| Kapetanios et al. (2012) | VAR | +1.5% |
| Baumeister & Benati (2013) | TVP-VAR | +3% |

### Effects on Inflation

| Study | Inflation Impact |
|-------|-----------------|
| Bridges & Thomas (2012) | +1 percentage point |
| Kapetanios et al. (2012) | +1.25 percentage points |
| Baumeister & Benati (2013) | +2 percentage points |

### Counterfactual Analysis

Baumeister and Benati (2013), conditioning on Charlie Bean's estimate that QE reduced long-term spreads by 50 basis points:
- Without QE, UK inflation would have fallen to -4% (annualized)
- Output growth would have dropped by 12% at annual rates

(source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)

## Effects on Bank Lending

QE had minimal effects on bank lending because UK banks were poorly capitalized and focused on deleveraging during the crisis period. The credit channel was impaired. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

The **Funding for Lending Scheme** (2012) was later introduced to specifically target bank lending incentives.

## Effectiveness Over Time

Evidence suggests QE1 had stronger effects than subsequent rounds:
- Markets were more distressed in 2009
- Yield levels were higher, giving more room for compression
- Later rounds may have been partially anticipated

However, this doesn't necessarily mean diminishing returns - the "surprise" element was simply larger in QE1. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)
