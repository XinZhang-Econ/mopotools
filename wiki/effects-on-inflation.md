# Effects of QE on Inflation

**Summary**: QE has had modest positive effects on inflation, typically estimated at 0.5-2 percentage points (peak effect). Effects were smaller than critics feared - fears of hyperinflation proved unfounded. The inflation response has been weaker than the output response, particularly in Japan where deflationary expectations remained entrenched.

**Research classification**: Empirical

**Sources**: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md, ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md, dell-ariccia-et-al-2018-unconventional-monetary-policies-in-the-euro-area-japan-and-the-united-kingd.md, ecbwp1956-pdf.md

**Last updated**: 2026-04-21

## Overview

A primary goal of [[quantitative-easing]] at the zero lower bound is to raise inflation (or prevent deflation). Central banks feared that without additional stimulus, economies would fall into deflationary spirals. The empirical record shows QE had positive but modest inflation effects.

## Transmission to Inflation

QE affects inflation through:

1. **Demand channel**: Higher output raises resource utilization and price pressures
2. **Expectations channel**: QE signals commitment to higher inflation, raising expected inflation
3. **Exchange rate channel**: Currency depreciation raises import prices
4. **Asset price channel**: Higher asset prices boost wealth and spending
5. **Credit channel**: More lending enables more spending

## United States

### Estimates

Bernanke (2012) noted that while QE substantially boosted output, the inflation effects were more modest given significant economic slack. (source: bernanke20120831a-pdf.md)

### Counterfactual: Deflation Averted

Baumeister and Benati (2013) found that without QE:
- Inflation would have fallen to **-1% (annualized)** in 2009Q2
- The economy faced significant **deflation risk** for two quarters
- The fraction of counterfactual draws with negative inflation peaked at **90%**

In other words, QE likely prevented deflation rather than causing high inflation. (source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)

### Actual Inflation Outcomes

Despite massive balance sheet expansion:
- Core PCE inflation averaged ~1.5% during QE period
- Remained below the Fed's 2% target
- Critics' predictions of runaway inflation proved unfounded

## United Kingdom

### Estimates

| Study | Method | Inflation Impact |
|-------|--------|-----------------|
| Bridges & Thomas (2012) | Monetarist model | +1 percentage point |
| Kapetanios et al. (2012) | VAR | +1.25 percentage points |
| Baumeister & Benati (2013) | TVP-VAR | +2 percentage points |
| Weale & Wieladek (2016) | VAR | +0.32% per 1% GDP in purchases |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Counterfactual

Baumeister and Benati (2013) estimated that without BoE QE:
- Inflation would have fallen to **-4% (annualized)**

(source: ijcb-v9n2-unconventional-monetary-policy-and-great-recession-estimating-macroeconomic-effects-spread.md)

### Actual Inflation Outcomes

UK inflation was above target during several years after QE, but this reflected:
- VAT increases (fiscal policy)
- Sterling depreciation
- Energy price increases

Core inflation was more muted.

## Euro Area

### Estimates

| Study | Program | Inflation Impact |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| Andrade et al. (2016) | APP | +0.4% (actual), +0.45% (expectations) |
| Altavilla et al. (2014) | OMT | +1.2% (Italy), +0.74% (Spain) |
| Mouabbi & Sahuc (2016) | APP/TLTRO | +0.25% (2014-16 average) |
| Cova et al. (2015) | APP | +0.8% |
| Hutchinson & Smets (2017) | Combined | +0.5% (cumulative 2016-19) |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Inflation Expectations

A key finding is that the ECB APP helped **reanchor** long-term inflation expectations:
- Long-term expectations had drifted below target during 2014
- APP announcement reversed this decline
- This "reanchoring channel" may account for ~1/3 of inflation impact

(source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md)

### Actual Outcomes

Euro area inflation remained stubbornly below the ECB's "below but close to 2%" target throughout the APP period.

## Japan

### Estimates

| Study | Program | Inflation Impact |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| De Michelis & Iacoviello (2016) | 2% target | +0.8% |
| Kan et al. (2016) | QQE1 | +0.3% to +1.5% |
| Michaelis & Watzka (2017) | QQE | +0.2% per 1% reserves |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### The Japan Puzzle

Japan's experience illustrates the limits of QE for raising inflation:

**Initial success**: Inflation rose to ~2% briefly in 2014

**Subsequent failure**: Inflation fell back to near zero by 2015, despite:
- Unprecedented asset purchases (>¥80 trillion/year)
- Negative interest rates
- Yield curve control

### Why Did Japan Struggle?

1. **Entrenched expectations**: Decades of deflation created a "deflationary mindset"
2. **Credibility deficit**: Past premature tightening damaged BoJ credibility
3. **Structural factors**: Aging demographics, wage rigidity
4. **Oil prices**: 2014-15 oil price collapse reversed gains

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## Sweden

### Estimates

| Period | Program | Inflation Effect | Source |
|--------|---------|-----------------|--------|
| 2015-2019 | Negative rates + QE | +20-50 basis points | Kolasa, Laséen & Lindé (2025) |
| 2020-2023 | Covid-era QE | +0.25 percentage points | Akkaya et al (2023) |

CPIF inflation returned to the 2% target by 2018-2019 after persistent undershooting in the early 2010s. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

### Inflation Expectations

Survey-based inflation expectations showed:
- Negative trend prior to 2015
- Reversed with introduction of unconventional policies
- 2- and 5-year expectations stabilized around 2%

The evidence is consistent with unconventional policies successfully reanchoring long-term inflation expectations in Sweden. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

### Exchange Rate as Primary Channel

Structural modeling (Kolasa, Laséen & Lindé 2025) finds that the inflation effect in Sweden operated primarily through **exchange rate depreciation**:
- QE led to real depreciation of the krona
- Depreciation raised import prices
- This was the dominant channel for inflation impact in a small, open economy

This differs from larger economies where domestic demand channels may be more important. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

### The 2022 Inflation Surge

Sweden experienced a sharp inflation surge in 2022 (CPIF reaching 10.2% in December 2022). The Riksbank's own analysis attributes this mainly to:
- Global supply disruptions
- Energy and food price shocks
- External spillovers from other economies

Domestic demand pressures contributed very little according to DSGE decomposition. Notably, Sweden's coordinated wage-setting framework (Industrial Agreement) helped dampen wage-price spiral risks. (source: riksbank-evaluation-12012026-pdf.md)

See [[qe-sweden]] for details.

## Cross-Country Comparison

### Conditions for Inflation Impact

QE has larger inflation effects when:

1. **Output gap is small**: More resource pressure translates to prices
2. **Expectations are anchored near target**: QE can nudge expectations up
3. **Exchange rate depreciates**: Import price channel operates
4. **Central bank is credible**: Markets believe the inflation target

### Why No Hyperinflation?

Critics warned QE would cause runaway inflation. It didn't because:

1. **Economic slack**: Large output gaps meant little resource pressure
2. **Base money ≠ inflation**: Banks held excess reserves rather than lending
3. **Expectations anchored**: Long-term expectations remained near target
4. **Global disinflationary forces**: Technology, globalization, demographics

## The Inflation Response Puzzle

A notable finding is that inflation responded less to QE than output:

- GDP effects: 1-3%
- Inflation effects: 0.3-2 percentage points

This may reflect:

1. **Phillips curve flattening**: Inflation less responsive to output
2. **Anchored expectations**: Long-term expectations didn't shift much
3. **Global factors**: Import prices, supply chains constrained domestic inflation
4. **Measurement issues**: Services inflation harder to capture

## Inflation Expectations

QE affected both actual and expected inflation:

### Survey Evidence

- US: Long-term expectations remained stable near 2%
- UK: Expectations were somewhat elevated but stable
- Euro area: APP helped reverse decline in expectations
- Japan: Expectations rose to ~1% but not to 2% target

### Market-Based Measures

Break-even inflation rates (from TIPS) responded to QE announcements, suggesting markets expected higher inflation.
