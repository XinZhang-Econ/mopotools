# Quantitative Easing: Euro Area

**Summary**: The ECB deployed multiple unconventional policy tools including LTROs, the Securities Markets Programme, Outright Monetary Transactions, and the expanded Asset Purchase Programme. The APP, launched in 2015, eventually reached €2.6 trillion EUR (~23% of euro area GDP) in holdings.

**Research classification**: Empirical

**Sources**: ecbwp1956-pdf.md, dell-ariccia-et-al-2018-unconventional-monetary-policies-in-the-euro-area-japan-and-the-united-kingd.md, four-stories-of-quantitative-easing-pdf.md

**Last updated**: 2026-04-21

## Background

The euro area faced a unique challenge: the Global Financial Crisis was compounded by a sovereign debt crisis in peripheral member states (Greece, Ireland, Portugal, Spain, Italy). Real GDP contracted by approximately 6% from peak to trough. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

The ECB's response evolved through three distinct phases:
1. **2008-2009**: Bank liquidity support (LTROs)
2. **2010-2012**: Sovereign debt crisis interventions (SMP, OMT)
3. **2014-2018**: Large-scale asset purchases (APP) and negative rates

## ECB Programs

### Longer-Term Refinancing Operations (LTROs)

**Initial LTROs (2008-2009)**: Extended maturity operations (3, 6, 12 months) at fixed rates with full allotment to restore interbank market functioning.

**Three-Year LTROs (December 2011, February 2012)**: €1 trillion EUR (~10% of euro area GDP) in ultra-long-term lending to banks.

**Rationale**: Euro area firms rely heavily on bank financing (~70% of external financing vs ~20% in the US), so supporting bank balance sheets was critical. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

**Note**: LTROs differ from QE in that they are loans to banks rather than outright asset purchases. However, banks used LTRO funds to purchase sovereign bonds, indirectly supporting yields.

### Securities Markets Programme (SMP)

**Announced**: May 10, 2010
**Purchases**: €218 billion EUR (~2% of euro area GDP) in Greek, Irish, Portuguese, Italian, and Spanish government bonds (held to maturity)

**Rationale**: "Address the malfunctioning of securities markets and restore an appropriate monetary policy transmission mechanism." (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

**Legal context**: Article 123 of the EU Treaty prohibits monetary financing of governments. Secondary market purchases were deemed permissible.

**Effectiveness**: Limited. Did not calm sovereign markets; crisis intensified through 2012.

### Outright Monetary Transactions (OMT)

**Announced**: September 6, 2012 (following Draghi's "whatever it takes" speech on July 26)

**Key features**:
- Unlimited potential purchases of 1-3 year sovereign bonds
- Conditionality: Country must request ESM program and accept monitoring
- ECB gave up seniority (unlike SMP)

**Impact**: OMT was never activated, yet the announcement alone dramatically reduced peripheral spreads. Krishnamurthy et al. (2018) estimated:
- 200 bps reduction in Italian and Spanish 2-year yields
- 500 bps reduction in Portuguese and Irish 2-year yields
- 1,000 bps reduction in Greek 2-year yields

The decline was attributed to: reduced default risk (37%), reduced redenomination risk (13%), and improved market liquidity (50%). (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Asset Purchase Programme (APP)

**Announced**: January 22, 2015
**Components**:
- Public Sector Purchase Programme (PSPP) - sovereign bonds
- Corporate Sector Purchase Programme (CSPP) - corporate bonds (from June 2016)
- Asset-Backed Securities Purchase Programme (ABSPP)
- Third Covered Bond Purchase Programme (CBPP3)

**Monthly pace**: Initially €60 billion EUR/month, varied between €30-80 billion EUR/month
**Total holdings**: ~€2.6 trillion EUR by end of 2018 (approximately 23% of euro area GDP)

**Purchase rules**:
- Market neutrality principle
- Capital key allocation across member states
- 33% issuer limit, 25% issue limit

### Negative Interest Rate Policy (NIRP)

**Announced**: June 5, 2014
**Deposit facility rate**: Cut from 0% to -0.1%, eventually to -0.5%

**Mechanism**: Banks charged for holding excess reserves, incentivizing lending.

## Macroeconomic Effects

### Effects on Sovereign Yields

The APP reduced long-term sovereign yields across the euro area:

| Study | Program | Yield Impact |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| Andrade et al. (2016) | APP | -45 bps average |
| Koijen et al. (2016) | APP | -2 to -60 bps (higher in distressed countries) |
| Altavilla et al. (2014) | OMT | -199 bps (Italy 2y), -234 bps (Spain 2y) |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Effects on GDP and Inflation

| Study | Program | GDP Impact | Inflation Impact |
|-------|---------|------------|------------------|
| Andrade et al. (2016) | APP | +1.1% | +0.4% |
| Altavilla et al. (2014) | OMT | +1.5% (Italy), +2% (Spain) | +1.2% (Italy), +0.74% (Spain) |
| Hutchinson & Smets (2017) | Combined | +1.7% (cumulative 2016-19) | +0.5% (cumulative) |

(source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Transmission Channels

The ECB APP operated through multiple channels (source: ecbwp1956-pdf.md):

1. **Duration risk channel**: Purchases removed interest rate risk from private portfolios
2. **Capital relief channel**: Higher bond prices improved bank capital positions
3. **Signaling channel**: Expectations of future short rates declined
4. **Reanchoring channel**: Long-term inflation expectations moved back toward target

Andrade et al. (2016) estimated the asset valuation channel explained ~2/3 of the inflation impact, with reanchoring explaining ~1/3.

### Cross-Country Heterogeneity

Effects varied significantly across member states. Peripheral countries (Italy, Spain, Portugal) experienced larger yield reductions than core countries (Germany, France). (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

## Special Considerations

### The "Whatever It Takes" Effect

Draghi's July 2012 speech is considered one of the most effective monetary policy interventions in history. Without making any purchases, the mere commitment to do "whatever it takes to preserve the euro" reversed the self-fulfilling dynamics that were driving peripheral yields higher. (source: dell-ariccia-et-al-2018)

### Bank vs. Capital Market Financing

The euro area's bank-centric financial structure meant ECB policy transmission differed from the US/UK. Supporting bank balance sheets (via LTROs and capital relief) was essential for monetary policy transmission. (source: four-stories-of-quantitative-easing-pdf.md)

### Legal Constraints

Article 123 of the EU Treaty constrained ECB actions. Programs required careful legal justification as secondary market operations distinct from prohibited monetary financing.
